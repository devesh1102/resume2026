At Microsoft, I was part of a 15-person team that had just 15 days to transition into a new Power Platform organization. The challenge was significant - we were all backend engineers, but now needed to deliver frontend work for a critical customer-requested feature in Power Automate's process mining capabilities. The team was intimidated by the massive React codebase and lacked fundamental frontend knowledge. This wasn't just a technical shift; it was make-or-break for our team's credibility in the new organization and delivering on customer commitments.

Given the urgency and complexity, my manager formally assigned me to lead the development of the OCPM overview page - a critical frontend feature that needed multiple widgets and sophisticated filter integration capabilities, all within a tight 2-month deadline. I was specifically responsible for not only delivering this high-visibility customer feature, but also for bridging my team's React knowledge gaps while maintaining our project momentum and code quality standards throughout the process.

I immediately took a strategic approach to tackle both the technical delivery and knowledge gaps simultaneously. First, I broke down the complex overview page into smaller, manageable sections and strategically assigned them to junior team members based on their current skill levels. I took ownership of the most challenging integration work myself, connecting all the individual components together, and personally implemented the core widgets that required advanced React knowledge.

Recognizing that React hooks were a major struggle area for the team, I conducted specialized training sessions focused specifically on this concept. I also brought in an experienced team member from my previous team to provide additional React training sessions, giving the team multiple learning perspectives. To make the concepts more tangible, I created practical dummy examples using LLM tools to demonstrate React patterns and usage in contexts similar to our actual work.

Throughout this process, I maintained rigorous code quality standards by thoroughly reviewing all team members' work and providing constructive feedback. I also completed basic React fundamental courses myself and actively shared those learnings with the team in real-time, showing them that we were all learning together. This approach helped build confidence while ensuring we met our quality standards.

The results exceeded our expectations on multiple fronts. We successfully delivered the OCPM overview page within our 2-month deadline, meeting all project requirements and quality standards. The feature performed exceptionally well and made such a strong impact that it was showcased at the Power Platform Community Conference - a significant recognition that demonstrated the quality of our work to the broader developer community.

More importantly, we delivered exactly what our customer had requested, directly enhancing Power Automate's process mining capabilities and strengthening our team's credibility in the new organization. The customer feedback was overwhelmingly positive, which was crucial for establishing our reputation in our new role.

From a leadership perspective, I'm particularly proud that every team member developed solid React fundamentals through our structured learning approach. The combination of hands-on experience with real project work and targeted training sessions gave them confidence to tackle future frontend challenges independently. Several team members later told me this experience transformed how they approached learning new technologies.

This experience taught me that effective leadership during transitions requires balancing immediate delivery pressures with long-term team development. By investing in my team's growth while maintaining high standards, we not only succeeded in the short term but built capabilities that served us well in subsequent projects.
